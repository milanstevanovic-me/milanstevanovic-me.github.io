About Me

View My Resume (PDF)

Growing up in Jacksonville, Florida, I was always the kid taking things apart just to figure out how they worked. That curiosity led me to Mechanical Engineering at the University of Florida.

I enjoy taking an idea from concept to reality from designing in SolidWorks and applying GD&T to thinking through manufacturing and how a design will perform. Through school and projects, I've worked with mechanical design, machining, 3D printing, and Arduino-based systems, including compressed-air-powered mechanisms.

More than anything, I like understanding why something works the way it does. I'm currently looking for a Summer 2027 mechanical engineering internship where I can keep learning, contribute to real projects, and challenge myself in a new environment. Outside of class, I'm involved with UF ASME and Gator Robotics.

Projects

A few projects from my coursework and independent work.

Air Engine Powered Food Slicer

Designed a compressed-air-powered food slicer with an adjustable, lockable slice mechanism, integrating with an existing flywheel/piston interface. Produced full manufacturing documentation under a $100 cost cap using DFMA principles.

Air Engine Manufacturing

Machined the flywheel, base, piston, and piston block of a compressed air engine on a manual mill and lathe, from part drawings revised with GD&T and DFM principles.

SolidWorks Robot Design

Designed a functioning robot with 20+ components in a team of 3, producing dimensioned 2D drawings with GD&T and a fully constrained 3D assembly, plus a video presentation on the design process.

Arduino Light-Tracking Servo

Built a closed-loop Arduino system using two photoresistors to detect and track a light source, continuously adjusting a servo's position in real time.
