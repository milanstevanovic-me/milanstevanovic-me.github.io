SolidWorks Robot Design

A fully constrained 20+ component robot assembly,
designed and documented for manufacturing.

← Back to Projects

Overview

Completed in a team of three for a CAD design course, this project involved designing a fully functioning mobile robot assembly from scratch in SolidWorks. The team split the robot into subsystems, with each member owning the modeling, mating, and drawing package for their assigned components before integrating everything into a single constrained assembly. The final deliverable included manufacturing-ready 2D drawings with GD&T callouts and a team video walkthrough of the design.

My Role

I owned the chassis, battery mounting system, and gear train. The chassis had to serve as the structural backbone for the entire robot — every other subsystem's components mounted directly to it, so I worked closely with teammates to nail down interface points (bolt patterns, mounting bosses, clearances) before finalizing my geometry. The battery mount needed to hold its component securely while staying serviceable, and the gear train had to transmit motion reliably within the space and tolerance constraints of the rest of the assembly. I produced the 2D drawings with GD&T callouts for all of my parts and contributed to the final video presentation.

Design Process

  • Modeled the chassis, battery housing, and gear train components in SolidWorks, designing each for manufacturability from the start
  • Coordinated mounting interfaces and clearances with teammates to ensure subsystems fit together without conflict in the final assembly
  • Constrained the gear train with appropriate mates to ensure correct meshing and rotation
  • Applied GD&T to critical dimensions on my drawings — including mounting hole positions and mating surfaces — to ensure parts would assemble correctly regardless of normal manufacturing variation
  • Ran interference checks after integration and iterated on chassis and mount geometry where parts didn't fit as designed
  • Contributed to the team video explaining our design decisions and process

Key Takeaways

This project sharpened my ability to design load-bearing structural components, work across a team where my parts had to interface correctly with subsystems I didn't control, and communicate design intent through properly toleranced drawings — skills that translate directly to real product development, where you're rarely the only person touching an assembly.
